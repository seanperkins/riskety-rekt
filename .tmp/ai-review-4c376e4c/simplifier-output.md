Reviewing from the Simplifier's lane: machinery vs. problem scope. The core architecture (pure engine, immutable per-day state, 7-step ordering) is genuinely load-bearing — steps 5→6 ordering and pending-escrow earn their keep, and the single-sink `reserves` is right-sized. Three pieces cost more to carry than they buy.

**1. DELETE the timing bonuses (Early Bird / Under the Wire).** This is the clearest over-engineering in the plan. It adds: an ordering rule over approval timestamps, tie semantics (two approvals sharing a stamp), a max-one-per-player constraint, and an *anti-staleness rotating exclusion* you've already anticipated needing but haven't specified. All of it earns **+2 soldiers/day** against a **4–7 baseline**, in a design that already concedes they're "small." The long paragraph defending "never to order submission" is itself a tell — a +1 micro-bonus shouldn't need that much justification. The stated requirement is accountability and a participation floor; the 2-action cap plus peer visibility already delivers that. Delete both bonuses. What's lost: up to +2/day for the "when you exercise" timing — which no goal asked for, and your own economy section argues is too small to matter. Lost: essentially nothing.

**2. DELETE Manifold as a "fallback source" adapter.** The failure mode section already gives you the right answer for a dead market source: *empty slate, post a note, carry on*. A fallback *adapter* (second network dependency, second fixture suite, second settlement format in the tests) is machinery standing in for an outage you already decided to survive by simply not publishing. Keep Manifold only as the *dev/test* source you hit from fixtures, not as a production pairing. What's lost: most-of-a-deploy's-worth of adapter surface for a day you've already accepted is "a disappointment, not fatal." Nothing of value.

**3. DELETE the `Scheduler` box from the architecture diagram.** It's a systemd timer, not an adapter. It appears in the ASCII diagram but has no entry in the Interfaces section and no methods — it's decorative. Drop it from the diagram. What's lost: nothing but a line of ASCII.

**Minor — not blocking:** the volume floor stored as *config* so it can be "raised without a deploy" reads as config-that-could-be-a-constant. Single droplet, single operator, sampled once and adjusted maybe per season. A constant you edit and redeploy (or even just a `.env`) is fine. Fixing this is a nit, not a blocker.

The property tests, season simulation, golden-file replay, and the deferred-economy-variant discipline are all well justified and I'd keep them untouched. The two substantive deletes (timing bonuses, market fallback) plus the diagram trim bring the plan to exactly the machinery the problem needs.

VERDICT: REVISE
